# Document E — Detailed Cinematic Storybook

**Last updated**: 2026-10-28
**Source**: `00-inbox/story.md`

35 of the 42 listed scenes are developed in full below (Parts I–IV, continuous).
The 7 undeveloped scenes are optional expansions identified in Document B and are
the designated trim material for reaching the 92-minute target; they contain no
source events.

Scene IDs: `PART.SECTION.CHAPTER.SEQUENCE.SCENE`. All action in present tense.

# PART I — THE ERRAND

---

## SCENE 1.1.1.1.1

**1. Title**: Cold Open — A Spot of Red
**2. Slug**: EXT. DEEP FOREST — DAY (time deliberately unplaceable)
**3. Story purpose**: State the film's image and its scale in one shot before any story exists: a very small red thing crossing a very large dark wood.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood (distant, unidentified).
**5. Opening visual**: Black. Then birdsong, and a slow fade into an immense static wide of forest — columnar trunks, no sky, no horizon.
**6. Setup**: No dialogue, no context. The audience is given a wood and asked to find something in it.
**7. Main action**: The frame holds. Nothing moves but leaf-shadow. Then, far back between two trunks, a point of red crosses left to right and is swallowed by a trunk. A beat. It reappears, smaller, further away. It is walking steadily, and it is alone. The frame holds until it is gone. Title card: **THE RED CAP**.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: None yet — the conflict is purely compositional: the figure versus the volume of the forest.
**9. Dialogue**: None.
**10. Emotional progression**: Calm → attentive → faintly uneasy.
**11. Visual storytelling**: The entire theme in one frame. Red = a child. Everything else = the world. The shot must be long enough to become uncomfortable.
**12. Sound**: Rich, layered birdsong. No music. Under the title card, the birdsong cuts to absolute silence — the film's first threat, placed before anything has happened.
**13. Props / costume**: The red cap only.
**14. Turning point**: The title. The audience now knows what to watch for.
**15. Closing image**: Empty forest. The red is gone.
**16. Transition**: Hard cut on the silence to the bright noise of the family cottage at dawn — relief.
**17. Duration**: 1.5 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 1 — the red cap; the forest.
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Visual interpretation.* Not an event in the source. Non-chronological in feel but not a flashback; it may be read as any one of her journeys.

## SCENE 1.1.1.1.2

**1. Title**: The Cap
**2. Slug**: INT. FAMILY COTTAGE — DAWN
**3. Story purpose**: Establish the girl, her mother, their world, and the cap as identity rather than costume. Plant the red thread motif.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood, Mother.
**5. Opening visual**: A needle pulling a red thread through red wool, in close-up, by candlelight.
**6. Setup**: Dawn. The mother has been up before her daughter, mending.
**7. Main action**: Mother repairs a small tear in the red cap. The girl comes down, still half asleep, and waits for it with obvious impatience — she wants it back the way one wants one's own face back. Mother bites off the thread, sets the cap on the girl's head, and ties it under her chin. A neighbour calls something through the half-door in passing — a greeting addressed not to a name but to "Czerwony Kapturek". Mother and daughter exchange a look: everyone calls her that. The girl is pleased by it.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Mother: finish the mending. Girl: be herself again. No conflict — this is the baseline of safety the film will spend 70 minutes removing.
**9. Dialogue**: Light and domestic. The key line is the neighbour's, which supplies her name without exposition.
**10. Emotional progression**: Sleepy → restored → proud.
**11. Visual storytelling**: Tying the cap under the chin is established here as the gesture she will repeat whenever she is uncertain (2.2.1.2.2, 3.2.1.2.2). The red thread is the only strong red besides the cap.
**12. Sound**: Hearth, needle, a kettle, chickens outside. The mother hums a folk melody — the film's theme, introduced here unremarkably.
**13. Props / costume**: Red cap, needle and red thread, linen dress, dark pinafore.
**14. Revelation**: Her name is the cap.
**15. Closing image**: The girl in the doorway, framed by morning, adjusting the knot.
**16. Transition**: Cut to a hand setting a letter — or a neighbour's words still hanging — on the table.
**17. Duration**: 3 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 1 — "Ponieważ nosiła zawsze czerwoną czapeczkę, wszyscy nazywali ją Czerwonym Kapturkiem."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Visual interpretation* of a single source sentence. The mending and the thread motif are invented staging; no plot is added.

## SCENE 1.1.1.1.3

**1. Title**: News of Grandmother
**2. Slug**: INT./EXT. FAMILY COTTAGE — EARLY MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: Deliver the inciting information — grandmother is ill — and establish the distance and the stakes.
**4. Characters**: Mother, Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: The mother standing very still in the open doorway with her back to us, listening to someone we do not see.
**6. Setup**: Word has come out of the forest that the grandmother has taken ill.
**7. Main action**: The mother closes the door and stands a moment. The girl asks. The mother tells her plainly — grandmother is sick, and there is no one out there with her. She goes to a shelf, takes down a small stoppered bottle, and weighs it in her hand. The girl watches her calculate: the distance, the day's work, her own mother alone in the middle of the wood. The girl says, before she is asked, that she can go. The mother does not answer.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Mother: find a way to do an impossible thing. Girl: be the solution. The conflict is entirely in the mother's silence.
**9. Dialogue**: Minimal. "Is she very sick?" — "She's alone." The refusal to answer the girl's offer is the scene's real line.
**10. Emotional progression**: Ordinary morning → worry → resolve.
**11. Visual storytelling**: The bottle in the mother's hand, held too long. The girl stepping into frame beside her, head barely to her shoulder — an image of how small the solution is.
**12. Sound**: The hum stops. Fire. Wind in the trees outside, heard through the door for the first time.
**13. Props**: Medicine bottle, cork, string.
**14. Decision**: The mother decides, without saying so, to send her daughter.
**15. Closing image**: Mother's eyes going to the window, to the treeline.
**16. Transition**: Match cut from the treeline to the basket on the table.
**17. Duration**: 3.5 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 1 — "mama Czerwonego Kapturka wysłała ją do chorej babci, która mieszkała w samym środku lasu."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Visual interpretation.* The source states the sending in one clause. The arrival of news and the mother's hesitation are invented staging; the facts (illness, isolation, the sending) are source.

## SCENE 1.1.1.2.1

**1. Title**: Packing
**2. Slug**: INT. FAMILY COTTAGE — MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: Establish the basket's exact contents — a continuity object and a running index of the errand's integrity.
**4. Characters**: Mother, Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: An empty wicker basket, overhead, on a scrubbed table.
**6. Setup**: The decision is made. Now it is practical.
**7. Main action**: In unhurried inserts, each item goes in: the medicine bottle wrapped in a cloth so it cannot break; bread; a comb of honey; a folded linen napkin laid over the top. The mother tells her how the medicine is to be given — a spoonful, no more. The girl repeats it back. She is proud to be trusted with a dosage. The mother folds the cloth, then unfolds and refolds it — the first visible crack in her composure.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Mother: delay. Girl: leave.
**9. Dialogue**: Instructions about the medicine, repeated back. Domestic, specific, warm.
**10. Emotional progression**: Busy → tender → reluctant.
**11. Visual storytelling**: The audience must be able to inventory this basket from memory; every later shot of it is measured against this one. The refolded cloth carries the mother's fear without a line.
**12. Sound**: Wicker, cork, paper, cloth. The hummed melody returns, thinner.
**13. Props**: Basket, medicine bottle, bread, honeycomb, linen cloth, a horn spoon.
**14. Revelation**: She has been given real responsibility — a measured dose.
**15. Closing image**: The napkin laid flat over the full basket, like a bed being made.
**16. Transition**: The girl lifts the basket; cut to the two of them at the door.
**17. Duration**: 4 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 1 — "Dziewczynka wzięła koszyk z lekarstwem i smakołykami."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Visual interpretation.* Contents beyond "medicine and treats" are invented and fixed here for continuity (Document D, rule 2).

## SCENE 1.1.1.2.2

**1. Title**: The Rule
**2. Slug**: INT./EXT. FAMILY COTTAGE — DOORWAY — MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: **Inciting incident completed.** Deliver the rule that the entire film will test, and make it an act of love rather than discipline.
**4. Characters**: Mother, Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: The mother kneeling to be at her daughter's eye level — the only time in the film she does this.
**6. Setup**: Basket packed, cap tied, door open on the morning.
**7. Main action**: The mother gives the instruction, and gives it twice. Straight along the road to grandmother's. Do not turn off it. Not for anything. The girl, who has been given a dosage to remember and is feeling very capable, agrees quickly — too quickly. The mother holds her shoulder until she says it back properly. She says it back. The mother still does not let go. Then she does.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Mother: transfer her fear into a rule small enough for a child to carry. Girl: be released.
**9. Dialogue**: The source's instruction, in plain words: *go by the road, straight to grandmother, and don't turn off it.* No elaboration about wolves — the film does not warn the audience either.
**10. Emotional progression**: Light → serious → held → released.
**11. Visual storytelling**: Hand on shoulder; the hand opening. The mother's eye-line goes past her daughter to the treeline during the second repetition.
**12. Sound**: Very quiet. Breath. Birdsong from the wood, inviting.
**13. Props**: Basket, cap.
**14. Decision**: The rule is given and accepted. Everything after this is the cost of it.
**15. Closing image**: The girl nodding, entirely sincere.
**16. Transition**: She turns and walks out of frame; hold on the mother.
**17. Duration**: 3.5 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 2 — "mama przestrzegała, żebym szła drogą prosto do babci i nie zbaczała z drogi" (reported later by the girl; staged here in its proper place).
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Reordering.* The source delivers the warning retrospectively, inside the girl's own thought on the meadow. The film dramatises it at its chronological point so the audience experiences the promise before the breaking. The girl's later recollection (2.2.1.1.1) then becomes a callback.

## SCENE 1.1.1.2.3

**1. Title**: The Gate
**2. Slug**: EXT. FAMILY COTTAGE — YARD AND TREELINE — MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: Cross the first threshold. End Part I.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood, Mother.
**5. Opening visual**: The gate latch, close, lifting.
**6. Setup**: Twenty metres of bright grass between the fence and the forest.
**7. Main action**: The girl goes through the gate and walks the open ground. Halfway, she turns and waves — big, two-armed, delighted. Her mother waves back. The girl walks on. The camera stays with the mother, who keeps waving after the girl has stopped looking. The red cap reaches the treeline, and the trees take her: in one step she goes from fully lit to entirely in shadow. The gate swings and knocks shut behind her on its own.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Girl: begin. Mother: watch until she cannot.
**9. Dialogue**: None. The wave is the line.
**10. Emotional progression**: Excitement → affection → dread (the mother's, and ours).
**11. Visual storytelling**: The single step from sunlight into shade is the most important cut-point in Part I — it must be a continuous shot, not an edit, so the audience feels it as a crossing rather than a scene change. The gate closing on its own is the film's first ominous object.
**12. Sound**: Grass, the gate's knock, the hummed melody now carried by the girl herself, going quieter with distance until the forest takes it too.
**13. Props**: Gate, basket, cap.
**14. Turning point**: She is in the forest and alone.
**15. Closing image**: The mother alone in the yard, hand still half raised.
**16. Transition**: Cut forward — boots, moving fast, on packed earth.
**17. Duration**: 2.5 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 1 — "pomachała mamie i ruszyła w drogę."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Faithful adaptation*, expanded to a full threshold beat.

# PART II — OFF THE PATH

---

## SCENE 2.1.1.1.1

**1. Title**: Fast Along the Path
**2. Slug**: EXT. FOREST PATH — MID-MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: Establish competence and pleasure, and train the audience to read the path as a continuous visible line.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: Boots, low angle, striking packed earth at a brisk, happy rhythm.
**6. Setup**: An hour into the wood. Birch and light. The forest at its friendliest.
**7. Main action**: She walks fast and well, basket swinging in the crook of her left arm. She talks to herself — recites the dosage, reports to an imaginary grandmother, assigns opinions to trees. She hops a root. She sings a few bars of the hummed melody. A wide shot: the path runs dead straight to a vanishing point and she is a red dot in the middle of it, entirely in the right place.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: She is doing exactly what she was told, enjoying it. The only tension is the audience's memory of the cold open.
**9. Dialogue**: Running self-narration; charming, not cute. Establishes her as a person who explains things out loud — the trait that destroys her in 3.1.1.1.2.
**10. Emotional progression**: Eager → joyful → settled.
**11. Visual storytelling**: **Rule established**: while she is on the path, the path's vanishing point is in frame. The audience learns this without noticing, so that its absence in 2.2.1.2.2 registers as panic.
**12. Sound**: Steady birdsong, boots, her voice, the melody. Warm and full.
**13. Props**: Basket, cap.
**14. Beat**: None — this scene's function is baseline.
**15. Closing image**: Her back, receding, the path holding her exactly in its centre.
**16. Transition**: Cut to a shallow stream crossing the path.
**17. Duration**: 2.5 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 2 — "Czerwony Kapturek szedł szybko leśną ścieżką."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Visual interpretation* of one clause. No invented events.

## SCENE 2.1.1.1.2

**1. Title**: The Last Landmark
**2. Slug**: EXT. FOREST PATH — STREAM CROSSING — MID-MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: Mark the edge of the known world; plant the forester.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood; the Forester (background, unremarked).
**5. Opening visual**: Stepping stones in brown water.
**6. Setup**: The stream is the last place she has been before. Beyond it, the trees get older.
**7. Main action**: She crosses carefully, holding the basket high, and soaks one hem anyway. On the far bank she stops and looks back at the stream for a moment — a small, private acknowledgement that she is now further from home than she has ever been by herself. She touches the knot of her cap. Then she goes on. Far behind her, almost out of focus between the trunks, a man in a green-brown coat with an axe over his shoulder crosses the path and is gone. She does not see him. The film does not point at him.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Girl: be brave about a boundary nobody set.
**9. Dialogue**: One line, to herself, about how grandmother must do this crossing in the dark.
**10. Emotional progression**: Confidence → a small hesitation → resolve.
**11. Visual storytelling**: The forest's register changes across this cut — birch to old columnar trunks, light to shade. The forester is staged at the very back of the frame and is never mentioned again until Part IV.
**12. Sound**: Water. The birdsong noticeably thinner on the far bank.
**13. Props**: Basket, wet hem (continuity), the forester's axe.
**14. Beat**: She passes the limit of the familiar.
**15. Closing image**: Her boots leaving wet prints that stop after four steps.
**16. Transition**: She walks on; a warm light begins to bleed in from frame left.
**17. Duration**: 2.5 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 2 (the journey); the forester from para. 7.
**19. Adaptation notes**: **Proposed change — requires approval.** Planting the forester here is not in the source, where he appears only at the end. The purpose is to make the rescue feel like the forest's own order rather than a deus ex machina. The alternative is to cut him and accept the source's abruptness — which is, in fairness, how folk tales work. Both readings are defensible; see Document H, Q3.

## SCENE 2.1.1.2.2

**1. Title**: The Meadow Revealed
**2. Slug**: EXT. FOREST PATH / EDGE OF THE FLOWER MEADOW — LATE MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: Make the temptation real. If the audience does not want to go in, the film fails here.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: Her face, stopping mid-stride, lit from the side by something out of frame.
**6. Setup**: A break in the canopy to her left.
**7. Main action**: She stops. The reverse: a clearing knee-deep in flowers, hazed with light and insects, so bright after the shade that the frame momentarily blows out and has to settle. She does not move for a long moment. She looks up the path — straight, dark, correct. She looks back at the meadow. She stays on the path. She takes three more steps along it. She stops again.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Errand versus wonder — and crucially, she resists first. The film must show her keeping her promise before it shows her breaking it.
**9. Dialogue**: None. A single indrawn breath.
**10. Emotional progression**: Purposeful → astonished → torn.
**11. Visual storytelling**: Shot grammar of temptation: the path shot is deep, straight, cool and composed; the meadow shot is shallow, warm, hazy and uncomposed. The cut between them is the choice. The three extra steps are the scene's best beat.
**12. Sound**: As she faces the meadow, the forest's birdsong is replaced by a thick, warm, enveloping insect hum. Nobody — including the audience — notices the birds have stopped.
**13. Props / environment**: Flowers (fix species here for continuity), low haze, drifting seed-heads.
**14. Turning point**: She stops a second time. She is going to go.
**15. Closing image**: Her shoulders turning towards the light.
**16. Transition**: Cut, on the turn, to her boot at the path's edge.
**17. Duration**: 2 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 2 — "aż dostrzegł polankę pełną kwiatów."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Faithful adaptation*, expanded. The initial resistance is invented and strengthens the source's own logic (she reasons before she strays).

## SCENE 2.2.1.1.1

**1. Title**: One Step Off
**2. Slug**: EXT. EDGE OF THE FLOWER MEADOW — LATE MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: **First major turning point.** The decision, spoken aloud, in the source's own reasoning.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: Extreme close: one boot on packed earth, the other lifting.
**6. Setup**: She is making a case to herself, and winning it.
**7. Main action**: She argues it out loud, exactly as the source has her think it: grandmother loves flowers, and will certainly be glad of a bouquet. Then the counter-argument, in her mother's words, which she repeats accurately and with full awareness — go by the road, don't turn off. Then the resolution: *nothing will happen if I stop for a moment, just here, on this meadow, to pick a few flowers.* The reasoning is sound. She believes it. She steps off the path. The sound of her footfall changes from hard to soft.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: The entire film's conflict, resolved wrongly, by a good child for a good reason.
**9. Dialogue**: The source's interior monologue rendered as her habitual thinking-aloud — established in 2.1.1.1.1 precisely so it is available here.
**10. Emotional progression**: Longing → negotiation → permission → delight.
**11. Visual storytelling**: Do not shoot this as naughtiness. Shoot it as generosity. The last shot of the path, held one beat after she leaves it, is the film's point of no return — and the path will not be seen again until 4.2.2.1.1.
**12. Sound**: **The single most important sound cut in the film**: packed earth → grass. Hard → soft. Instruction → freedom.
**13. Props**: Basket (shifted to the other arm, hands now free for picking).
**14. Decision**: She leaves the path.
**15. Closing image**: The empty path, holding, with the sound of her moving away through grass off-screen.
**16. Transition**: Cut to flowers filling the frame.
**17. Duration**: 2 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 2 — the girl's full interior monologue, and para. 3 — "Dziewczynka zeszła ze ścieżki."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Faithful adaptation.* Interior thought converted to spoken reasoning, justified by the trait established in 2.1.1.1.1. This is the most literal scene in the film and should remain so.

## SCENE 2.2.1.2.1

**1. Title**: Better and Better
**2. Slug**: EXT. FLOWER MEADOW — LATE MORNING
**3. Story purpose**: Stage the drift — the source's "the further she went, the more beautiful the flowers were" — as an invisible mechanism.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: Her hands closing on a stem; the snap; the flower added to a growing bunch.
**6. Setup**: Minutes passing, pleasantly.
**7. Main action**: She picks. She sighs over them — they are so beautiful, and they smell so wonderful. Each time she straightens, there is a better flower a few steps away: a deeper colour, a taller stem, a perfect one. She goes to it. And to the next. The scene is built as a chain of small, entirely reasonable movements, each of which is nothing. She kneels, gets grass on her pinafore and a burr on her hem. She arranges and rearranges the bunch. Twice the camera pulls wide and she is further from where she was than the scene has let us feel.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Girl: make the best possible bouquet. The antagonist here is not the wolf — it is the next flower.
**9. Dialogue**: Delighted fragments to herself, and to the flowers.
**10. Emotional progression**: Pleasure → absorption → trance.
**11. Visual storytelling**: Shoot almost entirely in close-up and shallow focus, so the audience is as spatially ignorant as she is. The two wides are the only honest information, and they are brief. No shot in this scene may contain the path.
**12. Sound**: The insect hum, thick and warm, with the slow drop-out of every other layer. It should be almost soporific.
**13. Props / costume**: Growing bouquet; grass stain and burr (continuity from here on); basket set down and picked up again — note where.
**14. Beat**: She stops noticing distance.
**15. Closing image**: An overhead: a small red dot, far from the dark line of trees where she entered.
**16. Transition**: Cut in, tight, on the bouquet being tied.
**17. Duration**: 4 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 3 — "Zaczęła zrywać kwiaty... Im dalej szła, tym kwiaty były piękniejsze. Nawet nie zauważyła, kiedy oddaliła się od drogi i od polany."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Faithful adaptation.* The "chain of small reasonable steps" is the cinematic translation of the source's single sentence and is the film's thesis about how children get lost.

## SCENE 2.2.1.2.2

**1. Title**: The Bouquet Is Finished
**2. Slug**: EXT. FOREST / FAR SIDE OF THE MEADOW — MIDDAY
**3. Story purpose**: The consequence lands: she is away from both the road and the clearing.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood.
**5. Opening visual**: The finished bouquet, held up and admired, filling the frame.
**6. Setup**: The job is done. Now she will go back.
**7. Main action**: She ties the stems with a length of grass, pleased. She lowers the bouquet — and the frame opens behind it. She is not on the meadow. She is among trees, standing in long grass, with no clearing and no path in any direction. Her smile goes. She turns a slow full circle. Every direction looks the same. She touches the knot of her cap. She does not cry and she does not call out; she picks a direction with visible effort and decides it is the right one. She takes a breath and settles the basket. She decides to go back.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Girl: find the way back. Obstacle: a forest with no features.
**9. Dialogue**: One small line to herself — that it was this way. It is a guess and she knows it.
**10. Emotional progression**: Satisfaction → disorientation → fear → managed resolve.
**11. Visual storytelling**: The reveal must be a *reframe, not a cut* — the bouquet lowering out of frame to disclose the world behind it. The slow 360° is the payoff for having withheld wide shots. The light has gone flat and the colour has drained; the cap is now the only saturated thing on screen.
**12. Sound**: The insect hum thins to nothing across the turn. By the end of the circle there is no birdsong, no insects, no wind. Room tone of an outdoor space, which is wrong, and should feel wrong.
**13. Props**: Bouquet (count and species locked here), basket, grass tie.
**14. Turning point**: She realises she is lost, and chooses to act anyway — a genuinely brave beat that the film should not undercut.
**15. Closing image**: She turns on her heel to set off.
**16. Transition**: The turn continues directly into the next scene — no cut if possible.
**17. Duration**: 4 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 3 — "Dopiero, gdy bukiet był gotowy, postanowiła zawrócić."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Faithful adaptation.* The source is explicit that she is lost before the wolf appears; the film honours this and refuses to make the wolf the cause of her distress.

## SCENE 2.2.2.1.2

**1. Title**: He Is Simply There
**2. Slug**: EXT. FOREST / BEYOND THE MEADOW — MIDDAY
**3. Story purpose**: **Midpoint.** The wolf's entrance — the film's single most important staging decision.
**4. Characters**: Red Riding Hood, the Wolf.
**5. Opening visual**: Continuation of her turn — her shoulder passing camera.
**6. Setup**: She has turned on her heel to go back.
**7. Main action**: She completes the turn. The wolf is standing in front of her. He is not crouched, not creeping, not snarling; he is standing still, at ease, close, and has evidently been there for some time. She does not scream. She goes completely rigid. The two of them hold still, looking at each other, for far longer than is comfortable. He is very large. The top of her cap is level with his shoulder. He tips his head slightly, the way a polite adult does before speaking.
**8. Objectives / conflict**: Wolf: assess. Girl: understand what she is looking at. Neither moves.
**9. Dialogue**: None in this scene. He speaks on the cut.
**10. Emotional progression**: Purpose → shock → total stillness → the held breath before he speaks.
**11. Visual storytelling**: **No music, no sting, no sudden cut.** He must simply be in the frame when the frame comes round. The horror is that the film does not treat it as horror. Shoot him from her eye-line (looking up, he fills frame) and her from his (she is tiny, centred, with the bouquet absurdly held out in front of her like an offering).
**12. Sound**: Absolute silence. Then, very quietly, her breathing. No score until Part IV.
**13. Props**: Bouquet held between them — accidentally, an offering.
**14. Turning point**: The story's two halves meet.
**15. Closing image**: His head tilting.
**16. Transition**: Straight cut on the tilt.
**17. Duration**: 1 min
**18. Source reference**: Para. 3 — "Obróciła się na pięcie, a tu nagle stanął przed nią wilk."
**19. Adaptation notes**: *Faithful adaptation.* The source's "nagle" (suddenly) is deliberately **not** rendered as a jump scare; the suddenness is the girl's, not the camera's. Flagged as a directorial principle in Document H, Q4.
