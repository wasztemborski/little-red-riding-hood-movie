## Part I — The Errand

- **Narrative purpose**: Establish the girl, her world, the red cap, the love behind the rule, and the forest as a boundary.
- **Central conflict**: A child's wish to be trusted versus a mother's fear.
- **Character development**: Red Riding Hood is established as capable, warm, and a little too open with strangers — set up for the disaster.
- **Plot milestones**: Grandmother is ill; the basket is packed; the rule is given; the gate is crossed.
- **Opening moment**: A spot of red moving through enormous grey trunks.
- **Closing moment**: The gate swings shut; the treeline closes behind her.
- **Runtime share**: ~20% (0:00–18:00)

## Part II — Off the Path

- **Narrative purpose**: Make the temptation beautiful and the transgression understandable, then make it irreversible.
- **Central conflict**: The promise versus the meadow.
- **Character development**: She reasons herself out of her own word — the most human moment in the film.
- **Plot milestones**: The journey; the meadow; the decision; the drift; the bouquet; the wolf.
- **Opening moment**: Sunlit path, confident stride.
- **Closing moment**: She turns on her heel; the wolf is there.
- **Runtime share**: ~25% (18:00–41:00)

## Part III — The Cottage

- **Narrative purpose**: The wolf's plan and its execution; sustained dread; the climax.
- **Central conflict**: A child's instinct that something is wrong versus her trust in a familiar room.
- **Character development**: Red Riding Hood's three questions — she *notices*, and names it.
- **Plot milestones**: The conversation; the directions given; the wolf's run; grandmother swallowed; the disguise; the questions; the leap; the sleep.
- **Opening moment**: "Good day, little girl."
- **Closing moment**: The wolf snoring under the quilt; the bouquet on the floor.
- **Runtime share**: ~37% (41:00–75:00)

## Part IV — The Forester

- **Narrative purpose**: Rescue, restoration, and the lesson earned rather than stated.
- **Central conflict**: Ordinary attentiveness versus an accomplished predator.
- **Character development**: The girl rejoins her path, now by choice.
- **Plot milestones**: The ajar door; the snoring; the window; the dispatch; the rescue; the thanks; the walk home; the coda.
- **Opening moment**: Boots on forest floor, an axe over a shoulder.
- **Closing moment**: Red cap on the path, the meadow glowing to one side, untaken.
- **Runtime share**: ~18% (75:00–92:00)

## Table of contents

### PART I — THE ERRAND (0:00–18:00)

#### Section 1.1 — A House at the Edge of the Wood
*Objective*: establish home, mother, cap, illness, rule. *Tone*: warm, bright, faintly anxious. *Turning point*: the rule is given and accepted. *Leads into*: the journey.

**Chapter 1.1.1 — Morning** (18 min)
*Central question*: Will she be trusted to go alone?
Dawn at the cottage on the forest's edge. The red cap is established as identity, not costume. A letter or neighbour's word brings news that grandmother is ill. The mother packs the basket with medicine and treats, hesitates over the distance, and sends her daughter — with a plain, repeated instruction to keep to the path. The girl promises, means it, and goes.

### PART II — OFF THE PATH (18:00–41:00)

#### Section 2.1 — The Path
*Objective*: the forest as wonder, the errand as pleasure, the girl as competent. *Tone*: adventurous. *Revelation*: she is further from home than she has ever been. *Leads into*: the meadow.

**Chapter 2.1.1 — Into the Wood** (9 min)
*Central question*: Can she do this on her own?
She walks fast and well, talking to herself, naming the landmarks her mother listed. The forest grows older and taller around her. She passes the last familiar marker. Birdsong thins. Then, to the left, light.

- Sequence 2.1.1.1 — *Good Walking* (5 min)

#### Section 2.2 — The Meadow
*Objective*: stage the central decision and its consequence. *Tone*: enchanted, then slowly wrong. *Turning point*: she leaves the path; later, she cannot find it. *Leads into*: the wolf.

**Chapter 2.2.1 — Flowers for Grandmother** (12 min)
*Central question*: Is a small broken promise still a broken promise?
Grandmother loves flowers. The girl argues the case to herself out loud and finds it convincing. She steps off. Each flower is more beautiful than the last; each is a few steps further. By the time the bouquet is finished, the meadow and the path are both behind her.

- Sequence 2.2.1.1 — *The Argument* (4 min)

**Chapter 2.2.2 — Turning on Her Heel** (2 min)
*Central question*: Which way is back?
She decides to return, turns — and the wolf is standing in front of her.

### PART III — THE COTTAGE (41:00–75:00)

#### Section 3.1 — What the Wolf Learns
*Objective*: the trap is set by courtesy. *Tone*: charming, then predatory. *Revelation*: he now knows the way. *Leads into*: the cottage.

**Chapter 3.1.1 — Good Day, Little Girl** (8 min)
*Central question*: Will she tell him?
The wolf is impeccably polite and asks where she is going. She tells him about the medicine, the illness, and — in careful, helpful detail — the location of the cottage. He expresses sympathy, smiles slyly, says goodbye, and disappears as fast as he appeared.

- Sequence 3.1.1.1 — *The Conversation* (8 min)
  - **3.1.1.1.1** Politeness — 3 min
  - **3.1.1.1.2** The Directions — 3 min
  - **3.1.1.1.3** The Smile and the Vanishing — 2 min

**Chapter 3.1.2 — He Gets There First** (14 min)
*Central question*: How much time does he have?
The wolf crosses the forest at speed and finds the cottage without difficulty. He knocks and enters before an answer. The grandmother is in bed, as described. He must be quick — the girl is already coming up the path. He swallows her, puts on her cap and glasses, and gets under the quilt at the last moment.

- Sequence 3.1.2.1 — *The Run* (6 min)
  - **3.1.2.1.1** Through the Forest — 3 min

#### Section 3.2 — Three Questions
*Objective*: the climax. *Tone*: intimate dread. *Turning point*: "To eat you." *Leads into*: the forester.

**Chapter 3.2.1 — Something Is Different** (10 min)
*Central question*: Why doesn't the room feel right?
The girl knocks and comes in. Only a head shows above the quilt. She sits down on the edge of the bed with the bouquet and the basket. Grandmother looks somehow unlike herself. The girl keeps talking, and keeps looking.

- Sequence 3.2.1.1 — *Arrival* (5 min)
  - **3.2.1.1.1** The Open Door — 2.5 min
  - **3.2.1.1.2** Sitting Down — 2.5 min
- Sequence 3.2.1.2 — *Unlike Herself* (5 min)
  - **3.2.1.2.1** The Bouquet — 2.5 min
  - **3.2.1.2.2** Looking Closer — 2.5 min

**Chapter 3.2.2 — Eyes, Ears, Teeth** (12 min)
*Central question*: What is in the bed?
The three questions and three answers. The wolf is briefly thrown by the first and recovers. On the third he gives up the pretence, leaps, and swallows her. Then he returns under the quilt and falls asleep.

- Sequence 3.2.2.1 — *The Questions* (7 min)

### PART IV — THE FORESTER (75:00–92:00)

#### Section 4.1 — The Man Who Was Passing
*Objective*: rescue by ordinary attention. *Tone*: tense, then joyous. *Turning point*: the window. *Leads into*: the walk home.

**Chapter 4.1.1 — Does Grandmother Snore Like That?** (6 min)
*Central question*: Will anyone notice?
A forester passes near the cottage, sees from a distance that the door is not properly shut, and hears loud snoring. Curious, he looks in through the window: a wolf, fast asleep in the bed. He understands at once.

- Sequence 4.1.1.1 — *Noticing* (6 min)
  - **4.1.1.1.1** The Half-Open Door — 2 min
  - **4.1.1.1.2** The Snoring — 2 min
  - **4.1.1.1.3** Through the Window — 2 min

**Chapter 4.1.2 — Out of the Belly** (6 min)
*Central question*: Are they alive?
He goes in and makes short work of the wolf. Grandmother and Red Riding Hood come out of his belly whole and unharmed. They embrace him and thank him for saving their lives.

- Sequence 4.1.2.1 — *Rescue* (6 min)
  - **4.1.2.1.1** Short Work — 2 min
  - **4.1.2.1.2** Whole and Healthy — 2 min
  - **4.1.2.1.3** Thank You — 2 min

#### Section 4.2 — The Road Home
*Objective*: restoration and the lesson. *Tone*: warm, settled, quietly wiser. *Leads into*: end of film.

**Chapter 4.2.1 — Setting the Room Right** (5 min)
*Central question*: What does recovery look like?
Grandmother is put back to bed properly. The medicine is finally given. The bouquet — crushed but intact — goes into water on the bedside table. The forester offers to walk the girl home.

- Sequence 4.2.1.1 — *The Medicine at Last* (5 min)
  - **4.2.1.1.1** The Spoon — 2.5 min
  - **4.2.1.1.2** Flowers in Water — 2.5 min

**Chapter 4.2.2 — On the Path** (5 min)
*Central question*: What has she learned?
The forester walks her home through the evening forest. They pass the meadow; she looks at it and keeps walking. Her mother is at the gate. Coda: a later day, the same path, the same red cap — not leaving it.

- Sequence 4.2.2.1 — *Escort and Coda* (5 min)
  - **4.2.2.1.1** Past the Meadow — 2.5 min
  - **4.2.2.1.2** Never Again Off the Path — 2.5 min
