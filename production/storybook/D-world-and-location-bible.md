# Document D — World and Location Bible

**Last updated**: 2026-10-28
**Source**: `00-inbox/story.md`

---

## Time period and setting

**Source facts**: A cottage at the edge of a forest; a forest with a path through
it; a meadow full of flowers; a cottage in the middle of the forest. No period,
country or season is stated.

**Proposed**: Central European, pre-industrial, indeterminate — roughly 18th–19th
century rural. Late spring or early summer (the meadow must be in full flower).
No roads, no vehicles, no firearms. Deliberately unplaceable: this is folk-tale
geography, not history. *Requires approval.*

## Locations

### L1 — The Family Cottage (edge of the wood)

**Source**: "na skraju lasu mieszkała mała dziewczynka."

Small timber house, vegetable plot, low fence, wooden gate. Beyond the gate,
twenty metres of open grass, then the treeline — an abrupt green-black wall.

- **Visual identity**: Warm, cluttered, hand-made. Daylight through small panes. Yellow, ochre, worn wood.
- **Emotional purpose**: Safety defined by its boundary. The gate is the film's first threshold.
- **Changes**: Dawn-lit at the start; at the end, lamplit at dusk, mother at the gate.
- **Scenes**: 1.1.1.1.2 – 1.1.1.2.3, 4.2.2.1.1

### L2 — The Path

**Source**: "szedł szybko leśną ścieżką"; the mother's instruction to go "drogą prosto".

A narrow, well-trodden track. Never ambiguous, never forked — the film's
moral spine made physical. It is always visible as a continuous line until the
moment the girl can no longer find it.

- **Visual identity**: A pale ribbon of packed earth, strongly lit, drawing the eye to a vanishing point.
- **Emotional purpose**: Order, instruction, safety. Its absence in 2.2.1.2.2 should be a genuine shock because the audience has been trained to see it in every frame.
- **Rule**: While the girl is on the path, the camera keeps the path's vanishing point in frame. From 2.2.1.1.1 it is not seen again until 4.2.2.1.1.
- **Scenes**: 2.1.1.1.1 – 2.1.1.2.1, 3.2.1.1.1, 4.2.2.1.1 – 4.2.2.1.2

### L3 — The Forest (Las)

**Source**: the grandmother lives "w samym środku lasu"; the wolf finds the cottage "bez trudu".

- **Visual identity**: Three registers. *Near* — birch and light, friendly. *Middle* — older, taller, columnar, quieter. *Deep* — enormous dark trunks, no horizon, no sky.
- **Emotional purpose**: Scale. The girl must be small in every wide shot.
- **Changes across the story**: Charming in 2.1.1, indifferent in 2.2.1, hostile in 3.1.2, and in Part IV simply a wood again — the change is in her, not in it.
- **Rule**: The wolf moves through the forest *off* the path and faster; the girl can only move slowly and on it. The film's suspense is this asymmetry.
- **Scenes**: 2.1.1.1.1 – 3.1.2.1.1, 4.2.2.1.1

### L4 — The Flower Meadow (Polanka pełna kwiatów)

**Source**: "polankę pełną kwiatów"; "Im dalej szła, tym kwiaty były piękniejsze"; she drifts away from both road and clearing.

A clearing of light in the middle of dark wood — a break in the canopy, knee-deep
in flowers, loud with insects.

- **Visual identity**: Blown-out warm light, haze, saturated colour, shallow focus. The only place in the forest that looks like a painting.
- **Emotional purpose**: Temptation that the audience must also feel. If the meadow is not genuinely beautiful, the film has no second act.
- **Critical design note**: The meadow has **no clear far edge**. It thins into the trees so gradually that neither the girl nor the audience can say where it ended. This is how the source's "nawet nie zauważyła" is staged.
- **Changes**: Radiant in 2.1.1.2.2; disorienting and too large in 2.2.1.2.2; in 4.2.2.1.1 seen from the path at dusk, still beautiful, not entered.
- **Scenes**: 2.1.1.2.2 – 3.1.1.1.3, 4.2.2.1.1

### L5 — Grandmother's Cottage (Chatka babci)

**Source**: "Chatka babci stała w środku lasu"; a door that can be knocked at and left ajar; a window that can be seen through; a bed with a quilt.

A single-room cottage, no neighbours, trees right up to the walls.

- **Exterior**: Low door, one front window, a woodpile. The door and the window are the two devices on which the whole fourth part turns — establish both clearly in 3.1.2.1.2.
- **Interior**: One room. Bed against the back wall, facing the door. A chair, a table, a stove. Jars of dried flowers on every shelf. Dim — light enters only through the front window and the open door, which means anyone in the bed is backlit and hard to read. This is the staging reason the girl cannot tell.
- **Emotional purpose**: Should feel like a destination and then like a trap, without changing a single prop.
- **Changes**: Ordinary and sickroom-quiet in 3.1.2.2.1; wrong in 3.2.1; violated in 3.2.2; restored and candle-lit in 4.2.1.1.
- **Scenes**: 3.1.2.1.2 – 4.2.1.1.2

## Colour and lighting concept

- **Part I**: Warm interiors, gold and ochre, soft morning. Red is intimate and small.
- **Part II**: Green and gold, rising saturation to a deliberate over-exposure at the meadow; then desaturation as she drifts, until the frame is green-grey and the cap is the only colour left.
- **Part III**: Cold shadow outdoors; indoors, a single hard window light against deep shade. High contrast, crushed blacks. Red against white quilt.
- **Part IV**: Low amber afternoon light entering the restored room; then blue-gold dusk on the walk home. Colour returns to the world.

Rule: **red belongs only to the cap** until Part IV, when it may appear again in
firelight and the mother's shawl.

Music: a single folk melody — hummed by the mother in 1.1.1.2.1, picked up by the
girl on the path, absent entirely from 2.2.1.2.1 to 4.1.2.1.2, and returned in
full orchestration at 4.2.2.1.2.

## Continuity requirements

1. **Light / time of day** progresses strictly dawn → dusk across one day. No scene may be lit out of sequence.
2. **The basket contents**: medicine bottle (stoppered, corked, string-tied), bread, honeycomb or cake, folded cloth. Establish exactly in 1.1.1.2.1 and match at every later open.
3. **The bouquet**: count and species fixed at 2.2.1.2.2. It is crushed in 3.2.2.2.1, found on the floor in 4.1.1.1.3, in water in 4.2.1.1.2 — same flowers.
4. **Grass stains and burr** on the girl's pinafore from 2.2.1.2.1 onwards.
5. **Nightcap and spectacles** are grandmother's in 3.1.2.2.1, the wolf's from 3.1.2.2.2, returned intact in 4.1.2.1.2.
6. **The cottage door** is left not fully closed by the wolf in 3.1.2.1.2 and stays that way until 4.1.1.1.3. It must be visible in every exterior between those points.
7. **The quilt** is pulled to chin height by the wolf and never lower until the leap.
8. **No injuries** anywhere in the film: the source says both come out "całe i zdrowe". No blood, no torn clothing beyond grass stains.
9. **Geography**: home → path → meadow (left of the path) → deeper forest → grandmother's cottage, further along the same path. The girl rejoins the path *before* the cottage, since the source has her arriving by it. The meadow must therefore lie between home and the cottage, on the left going out and the right coming back.
10. **The wolf's travel time** must read as shorter than the girl's: he leaves at midday and has finished by the time she knocks. Cross-cutting in 3.1.2 must keep this legible.
